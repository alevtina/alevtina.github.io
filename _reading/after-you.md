The story picks up about eighteen months after Will's death, with Lou still reeling and estranged from much of her family over her role in his choice. She's drifting through a subdued, sheltered life, working a dead-end job under a difficult boss, when a fall from her roof (one that some people assume was a suicide attempt) forces a reckoning. From there, she starts attending a grief support group, meets the paramedic who saved her, gets a surprise job offer in New York, and discovers Lily, a teenage daughter Will never knew he had. The plot could have gone in more obvious directions, like a lawsuit or a media firestorm, but instead it gives Lou room to grieve at her own pace while gradually pulling her back toward life.

I liked this one, though not as much as the first. Lou remains a great character: flawed, relatable, and someone I recognized pieces of myself in, and the writing is just as sharp and witty as before. But it doesn't quite reach the heights of the original, and I don't think it needed to exist as a standalone story. It felt more like the necessary follow-up to a book that had already been adapted into a movie than a story that demanded to be told. Still, if you loved Lou and her family the first time around, spending another few hundred pages with them is a worthwhile, easy read, even if it doesn't carry the same emotional weight.
