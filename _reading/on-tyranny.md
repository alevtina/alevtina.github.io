Snyder, a historian of the Holocaust, Nazi Germany, and the Soviet Union, distills what he learned from 20th-century authoritarian regimes into twenty short chapters, each built around one instruction for ordinary citizens. The first, “Do not obey in advance,” covers how much of an authoritarian’s power gets handed over voluntarily before anyone demands it. The others range from defending institutions and believing in truth to being a patriot (not a nationalist) and being as courageous as you can. At 126 pages, it can be read in a single sitting.

I read it nine years after publication, and it holds up. Snyder writes in short, direct sentences, and the historical examples make each lesson concrete. The first sixteen chapters or so are the tightest, and the last few lose some focus. It’s a quick read that has stayed with me.
